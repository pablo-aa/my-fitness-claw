# What we don't do

**Bottom line:** Popular rules that the evidence does not support, and the evidence-based alternative.
**Evidence:** each line carries its own source; ratings reflect the weight of that source.

## Nutrition

- **"Eat 6 small meals a day to boost metabolism."** 24 h energy expenditure does not differ between nibbling and gorging (Bellisle 1997), and the meal frequency meta-analysis "benefit" came from a single study (Schoenfeld 2015). Strong.
- **"Breakfast is the most important meal for weight loss."** In RCTs, breakfast eaters ate more and weighed slightly more than skippers (Sievert 2019). Moderate.
- **"Carbs at night make you fat."** Fat loss follows the energy deficit, not the clock (Aragon 2017); the one RCT testing carbs mostly at dinner found better, not worse, results (Sofer 2011). Weak but nothing supports the myth.
- **"Carbs and insulin make you fat; low-carb burns more fat."** At equal calories, low-carb diets do not produce more fat loss (Hall & Guo 2017, Hall 2015), and 12-month weight loss matches low-fat diets (Gardner 2018). Strong.
- **"There is one best diet."** Named diets give similar results; the best one is the one a person sticks to (Johnston 2014). Strong.
- **"Clean eating: some foods are forbidden."** Rigid rules are linked to overeating and cravings; flexible control predicts dieting success (Smith 1999, Meule 2011). Moderate.
- **"Starvation mode stops fat loss."** After an 8-week 1,000 kcal/day diet in adults with obesity, metabolic adaptation in resting metabolic rate was about 90 kcal/day and fell to about 40 once weight stabilized (Martins 2020). Moderate.
- **"The anabolic window: drink a shake within 30 minutes."** With total protein equal, timing around training does not change strength or hypertrophy (Schoenfeld 2013). Strong.
- **"The body can only use 30 g of protein per meal."** 100 g produced a larger and longer anabolic response than 25 g (Trommelen 2023). Moderate (acute study).
- **"High protein damages healthy kidneys."** Higher-protein diets did not change kidney function over time in healthy adults (Devries 2018). Moderate. People with kidney disease are a different case.
- **"You need animal protein to build muscle."** At adequate total protein, plant and animal sources give similar muscle gains (Hevia-Larraín 2021, Yimam 2026). Moderate.
- **"Detox diets clean out toxins."** No RCT has tested commercial detox diets in humans (Klein & Kiat 2015). Strong for lack of evidence.
- **"BCAAs build muscle."** No human evidence that BCAAs alone stimulate muscle protein synthesis (Wolfe 2017). Moderate.
- **"Fat burners and testosterone boosters work."** Outside caffeine and green tea, fat burner evidence is lacking (Jeukendrup & Randell 2011); most testosterone booster ingredients have no supporting data (Clemesha 2020). Moderate.

## Training

- **"Crunches burn belly fat" (spot reduction).** Six weeks of abdominal exercise did not reduce abdominal fat (Vispute 2011), and training one leg reduced fat elsewhere, not in that leg (Ramírez-Campillo 2013). Moderate; one small 2023 trial reported a modest local effect (Brobakken 2023), so the honest answer is that any local effect is small compared with overall fat loss.
- **"Muscle confusion: change exercises constantly."** Systematic, purposeful variation can help, but excessive random rotation may hinder gains (Kassiano 2022). Moderate.
- **"Light weights for toning, heavy for bulking."** Hypertrophy is similar across a wide range of loads when sets are taken to failure; heavy loads win only for max strength (Schoenfeld 2017). Strong.
- **"Cardio kills your gains."** Whole-muscle growth is not clearly impaired by adding aerobic training; at the fiber level there may be a small negative effect, mostly with running (Lundberg 2022). Moderate.
