# Exercise selection

**Bottom line:** Build on a few compound movements (a squat or leg press, a hinge, an upper push, an upper pull) and add isolation work where a muscle needs it.
Machines and free weights work equally well for muscle; pick by safety, skill, equipment and preference.
Use a full range of motion, with emphasis on the stretched position.
**Evidence:** moderate for equipment and range of motion; weak and contested for "long muscle length" being superior.

## Profile filters first

Apply these before any optimization.

- **Age and experience:** resistance training is safe for healthy adults of all ages; in more than 38,000 participants, including over 11,000 older adults, exercise did not increase serious adverse events (Currier 2026).
  The NSCA position statement recommends resistance training for older adults and covers considerations for frailty and sarcopenia (Fragala 2019).
  Starting older and novice lifters on stable, low-skill options (machines, dumbbells, leg press, supported rows) is a practical safety choice, not a tested requirement.
- **Injuries and pain:** substitute a pain-free variation or use a pain-free range of motion; partial ROM is a valid alternative when injury prevents full ROM (Wolf 2023).
- **Equipment:** machines, free weights and elastic bands all improve hypertrophy (Currier 2026; Haugen 2023), so home setups with dumbbells or bands are fine.
- **Skill:** high-skill lifts (barbell back squat, deadlift from the floor, Olympic lifts, pistol squats) are opt-in, not defaults; they are not required for muscle or general strength.

## Defaults

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| 5-6 exercises per session, mostly machines and dumbbells | Compound core (free weights or machines) plus 2-4 isolation exercises per session | Exercise choice by muscle and resistance profile, including lengthened-biased options |
| Same exercises for at least 8-12 weeks to learn them and track progress | Keep main lifts stable for a block; rotate accessories when they stall or bore | Rotate variations deliberately, not randomly |
| Full ROM the person can control without pain | Full ROM, emphasize the stretch | Full ROM or lengthened partials as an option |

## What the evidence shows

- Free weights vs machines: no difference in hypertrophy or in strength when each is tested fairly; strength is specific to the trained modality (Haugen 2023).
- The 2026 ACSM overview found equipment type and exercise complexity did not consistently change outcomes; full ROM enhanced strength (Currier 2026).
- Full vs partial ROM: a trivial overall advantage for full ROM, with a possible hypertrophy benefit for partials at long muscle lengths (Wolf 2023).
- Full ROM or partial ROM in the initial (stretched) part of the movement gave more growth in several muscles than partials in the shortened part (Kassiano 2023).
- Lengthened partials and full ROM produced similar upper-body hypertrophy in trained lifters (Wolf 2025).
- But a meta-analysis on mean muscle length found longer and shorter lengths produced similar regional hypertrophy (Varovic 2025), while a 2026 narrative review argues that training with meaningful load at long lengths helps growth (Pedrosa 2026).
- Lifting tempo has minimal effect on hypertrophy; use a controlled tempo (Enes 2025).
- Exercise order: strength is enhanced for exercises done at the start of a session (Currier 2026), so put priority lifts first.
- Women gain muscle at similar relative rates to men, so the same exercise logic applies (Refalo 2025).
- Time-efficient programs should include at least one leg press or squat pattern, one upper pull and one upper push (Iversen 2021).
- Natural bodybuilding recommendations: a core of multi-joint exercises plus single-joint exercises for specific muscles, full ROM and proper form (Helms 2015).

## When to go beyond the default

Maxxers targeting a lagging muscle can choose exercises that load it in the stretched position (e.g. overhead triceps extension, seated leg curl, deep-stretch flyes) or add lengthened partials at the end of a set.
Barbell competition lifts belong in the program when strength in those lifts is the goal.

## Caveats

- The "long length" literature is new, mostly in young adults, and the differences in muscle length between conditions were small (Varovic 2025); treat it as a tiebreaker, not a rule.
- Injury data in barbell sports show 1.0-4.4 injuries per 1000 hours in powerlifting (Tung 2024); general gym training in non-competitors is less studied.
