# Rest intervals

**Bottom line:** Rest long enough to do the next set with good reps: about 1-2 minutes for most exercises, 2-3+ minutes for heavy compound lifts.
Going beyond about 90 seconds adds little for muscle; very short rests mainly cost reps.
**Evidence:** moderate: several systematic reviews agree on the direction, but effects are small and studies are short.

## Defaults

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| Rest until breathing settles and you feel ready, usually 1-2 min | Compounds 2-3 min, isolation 1-2 min | Heavy compounds 2-4 min, isolation 1-2 min |
| No timer needed | Use a timer to avoid drifting to 5+ min | Supersets of non-competing muscles to save time |

## What the evidence shows

- A Bayesian meta-analysis (9 studies) found a small hypertrophy benefit for rest intervals longer than 60 seconds, and no appreciable difference beyond 90 seconds; training to failure or not did not change this (Singer 2024).
- Robust strength gains occur even with short rests (under 60 s), but longer rests (over 2 min) appear needed to maximize strength in trained people; in untrained people 60-120 s seems sufficient (Grgic 2018).
- The 2026 ACSM overview found strength was not affected by short (under 1 min) vs long (over 1 min) rest, and data were insufficient to judge rest effects on hypertrophy (Currier 2026).
- Natural bodybuilding recommendations: 1-3 minutes is adequate, longer can be used (Helms 2015).
- Supersets, drop sets and rest-pause roughly halve session time while keeping volume, likely better for hypertrophy than for maximal strength (Iversen 2021).

## When to go beyond the default

Short on time: pair exercises for different muscles (e.g. a row with a leg press) and rest between pairs.
Strength-focused maxxers can rest 3-5 minutes on top sets of heavy lifts.

## Caveats

- The likely mechanism is that short rest lowers the reps and load you manage in later sets (Singer 2024), so a person who keeps performance with shorter rest loses little.
- Sources disagree a little on strength (Grgic 2018 vs Currier 2026); both agree the effect is small.
- Most hypertrophy studies are in younger, untrained adults over 5-10 weeks (Singer 2024).
