# Frequency

**Bottom line:** For muscle growth, weekly volume matters and frequency mostly does not: it is a tool to spread sets across the week.
Training each muscle about twice a week is a sensible default; for strength, more frequent practice of a lift helps somewhat.
**Evidence:** strong for hypertrophy (volume-equated frequency makes little difference); moderate for strength.

## Defaults

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| 2-3 full-body sessions/week | 3-4 sessions/week | 4-6 sessions/week |
| Each muscle 2x/week | Each muscle 2x/week (full body or upper/lower) | Each muscle 2-3x/week; main lifts 2-3x/week if strength is a goal |
| Split: full body | Split: whichever the person prefers | Split: any, chosen to keep per-session volume manageable |

## What the evidence shows

- With volume equated, higher vs lower frequency made no meaningful difference to hypertrophy, including in trained people; non-equated studies favored higher frequency only modestly, likely because it added volume (Schoenfeld 2019).
- The 2026 ACSM overview found hypertrophy was not affected by frequency (1 vs more than 5 days/week) when total volume was equated, while strength was enhanced by ≥2 sessions/week (Currier 2026).
- Pelland 2026 found the effect of frequency on hypertrophy compatible with negligible, but a clear positive effect on strength, with diminishing returns.
- Strength gains rose with frequency, but not when volume was equated, so most of the benefit seems to come from extra volume (Grgic 2018).
- Split and full-body routines produced similar strength and hypertrophy at equal volume (Ramos-Campo 2024).
- Natural bodybuilding recommendations: train muscles 2x/week or more, using higher frequency when volume is high, so no single session gets excessive (Helms 2015).

## Split consequences in practice

- Full body 2-3x/week: covers every muscle twice with few sessions; best when time is short or for beginners.
- Upper/lower 4x/week: each muscle twice, shorter sessions than full body at the same volume.
- Body-part splits (each muscle 1x/week): work if volume is reached, but pack many sets into one session; the older meta-analysis favoring 2x over 1x (Schoenfeld 2016) was not confirmed once more studies were pooled (Schoenfeld 2019).
- A missed session in a body-part split costs a full week of stimulus for those muscles; in full body it costs less.

## When to go beyond the default

Raise frequency when weekly volume per muscle gets high, or when a strength lift needs more practice.
As a rough per-session guide, Helms 2015 suggests about 40-70 reps per muscle group per session (e.g. 4-6 sets of 10-12; the set conversion is ours).

## Caveats

- Most frequency studies are short and in untrained or recreationally trained young adults.
- The per-session guide above is expert recommendation, not a tested threshold.
