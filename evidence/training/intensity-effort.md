# Intensity and effort

**Bottom line:** For muscle, the load matters little as long as sets are hard: anything from about 5 to 30 reps works when taken close to failure.
For maximal strength, heavier loads win.
Stopping 1-3 reps short of failure gets nearly all of the benefit with less fatigue and discomfort.
**Evidence:** strong for load and hypertrophy; moderate for proximity to failure (estimated RIR, short studies).

## Defaults

RIR = reps in reserve: how many more clean reps you could have done.

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| 8-15 reps on everything | Compounds 6-12, isolation 10-20 | Compounds 3-12 (3-6 if strength matters), isolation 8-20+ |
| Stop when the next rep would be slow or form would break (about 2-3 RIR) | 1-3 RIR; 0-1 on the last set of isolation exercises | 0-2 RIR; failure mostly on isolation and machines, rarely on heavy barbell lifts |
| No numbers to track besides weight and reps | Note RIR on the last set | Log RIR per set and use it to adjust load |

## Load

- Hypertrophy was similar between low (≤60% 1RM) and high loads when sets went to failure, while 1RM strength favored heavy loads (Schoenfeld 2017).
- A network meta-analysis of 28 studies found no hypertrophy difference among low (>15RM), moderate (9-15RM) and high (≤8RM) loads to failure; strength favored high and moderate over low (Lopez 2021).
- Across 178 strength and 119 hypertrophy studies, loads above 80% 1RM maximized strength, and all prescriptions promoted hypertrophy comparably (Currier 2023).
- The 2026 ACSM overview: strength enhanced by heavier loads (≥80% 1RM) with a dose-response; hypertrophy not affected by load from 30% to 100% 1RM (Currier 2026).
- Very light loads (15-40 reps) work only if taken to failure, which is uncomfortable; 6-15RM is the practical sweet spot (Iversen 2021).

## Proximity to failure

- Training to failure vs non-failure: no significant difference for strength or hypertrophy overall, with a small hypertrophy edge for failure in trained people (Grgic 2022).
- Set failure vs non-failure gave a trivial hypertrophy advantage; momentary failure specifically showed no advantage (Refalo 2023).
- A dose-response meta-regression found hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure, while strength was similar across a wide range of RIR (Robinson 2024).
- The newest meta-analysis (20 studies) found non-failure as effective as failure for hypertrophy and slightly better for dynamic strength (Wu 2026).
- Failure sets felt worse (higher discomfort and exertion, less positive feelings) than 1-2 RIR in trained lifters (Refalo 2025).
- People misjudge RIR, underpredicting reps left by about 1 on average, with better accuracy at heavier loads, lower rep counts and closer to failure (Halperin 2022).
- Expert recommendation for natural lifters: limit failure on heavy, taxing exercises; use it mainly on single-joint exercises and higher reps (Helms 2015).

## When to go beyond the default

Advanced lifters chasing hypertrophy can take more sets to 0-1 RIR, especially on stable machine and isolation work.
Lifters with a strength goal (powerlifting, 1RM tests) should spend part of their training at 1-6 reps on the lifts they want to improve.
The RIR-based RPE scale is a useful tool for maxxers to autoregulate load (Helms 2016).

## Caveats

- In Robinson 2024, RIR was estimated by the authors from study descriptions, not measured, so exact optimal RIR values are not known.
- RIR estimates are imprecise regardless of training status (Halperin 2022); the pupil form-based stopping rule is a practical simplification, not a tested one.
- Most participants were young adults; data in older adults and highly trained lifters are limited (Grgic 2022).
