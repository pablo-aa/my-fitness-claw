# Volume

**Bottom line:** Count hard sets per muscle per week.
More sets build more muscle, but each extra set buys less than the one before, so start low and add sets only while progress justifies them.
**Evidence:** strong that a dose-response with diminishing returns exists; moderate for the exact set numbers.

## Defaults

A hard set is a working set taken within about 0-3 reps of failure (see [intensity and effort](intensity-effort.md)).
Warm-up sets do not count.

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| 4-10 hard sets per muscle per week | 10-15 | 12-20, more for a lagging muscle if recovery allows |
| Count only the big movements; no tracking per muscle | Count direct sets as 1 and indirect sets as 0.5 (e.g. bench press counts half for triceps) | Same counting, reviewed weekly against performance |
| Add sets only after weeks of consistent training | Add 1-2 sets per muscle when progress stalls and recovery is good | Add volume in small steps; remove it if performance or joints suffer |

## What the evidence shows

- Each additional weekly set was associated with a small extra gain in muscle size, and 10+ sets per muscle tended to beat fewer (Schoenfeld 2017).
- Multiple sets per exercise produced about 40% larger hypertrophy effect sizes than single sets (Krieger 2010).
- The 2026 ACSM position stand concludes that hypertrophy is enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets/week) (Currier 2026).
- The largest dose-response meta-regression so far (67 studies, 2058 participants) found gains in size and strength rise with volume, with diminishing returns, much more pronounced for strength than for size (Pelland 2026).
- The same analysis found that counting indirect sets as half a set ("fractional" method) predicted results best (Pelland 2026).
- In trained young men, 12-20 weekly sets matched more than 20 for quadriceps and biceps; only triceps showed an advantage for higher volume (Baz-Valle 2022).
- A time-efficiency review sets the floor at a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle (Iversen 2021).
- In older adults, low volume was the most effective for timed up and go, 6-minute walk, lean mass and muscle size, while higher volume helped strength (Radaelli 2025).

## When to go beyond the default

Go above ~20 weekly sets only for a specific muscle, for a block of weeks, when the person is advanced, recovering well and has stalled at lower volumes.
Strength-focused trainees gain less from extra volume than from heavier loads and practice of the lift (see [intensity and effort](intensity-effort.md)).

## Caveats

- Most studies used young adults (Pelland 2026: mean age about 25, 79% male) over weeks to a few months.
- Where volume stops paying off is not settled; very high volumes (20-30+ sets) show benefit in a few studies and none in others, and experts disagree.
- "Junk volume" is not a defined scientific concept; the practical version is that sets far from failure or done with poor technique probably add fatigue more than stimulus.
- Pelland 2026 is Bayesian and exploratory; do not read exact optimal numbers into it.
