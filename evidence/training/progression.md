# Progression

**Bottom line:** Do a bit more over time: more reps at the same weight, then more weight.
Double progression covers almost everyone; beginners progress fast for months, advanced lifters slowly, and complex periodization is optional.
**Evidence:** moderate: progressive training is the standing recommendation of position stands, but few studies compare progression schemes head to head.

## Defaults

Double progression: pick a rep range (e.g. 8-12).
Keep the weight until every working set reaches the top of the range at the target effort, then add the smallest available increment and start again near the bottom.

| pupil | grinder | maxxer |
|---|---|---|
| Double progression on every exercise, 8-12 or 10-15 reps | Double progression; compounds 6-10, isolation 10-15 | Double progression or RIR-based load selection (see [intensity and effort](intensity-effort.md)) |
| Expect weight increases most weeks in the first months | Expect progress over weeks, not every session | Expect progress over months; judge trends, not single sessions |
| If a lift stalls for 2-3 sessions, check sleep, food and technique before changing anything | If stalled for 2-3 weeks, swap the exercise variation or add 1-2 sets | Optional undulating periodization (varying heavy and moderate days) for strength goals |

## What the evidence shows

- ACSM recommends that healthy adults perform progressive resistance training, and lists periodization among variables that did not consistently change outcomes (Currier 2026).
- In trained lifters, adding reps at a fixed load and adding load at fixed reps produced similar hypertrophy and strength over 8 weeks (Plotkin 2022).
- With volume equated, periodized training gave somewhat more 1RM strength than non-periodized (ES 0.31), and undulating beat linear only in trained participants; hypertrophy did not differ (Moesgaard 2022).
- Advanced methods (clusters, drop sets, flywheels, etc.) showed no clear benefit over traditional training in untrained to moderately trained people (Cowley 2026).
- Strength gains slow sharply with time: in 14,690 adults on a minimal-dose program, gains were about 30-50% in the first year and had practically plateaued by 1-2 years (Steele 2023).
- Untrained participants show greater hypertrophy than trained ones, and trained participants benefit from more sessions (Lopez 2021).
- Natural bodybuilding recommendations favor a periodized approach for advanced trainees, with block and undulating models showing promise (Helms 2015).

## When to go beyond the default

When double progression stalls for several weeks despite good sleep, food and effort, the maxxer can add structured variation (heavy, moderate and light days) or volume blocks.
Before that, rule out the cheaper explanations: under-eating, poor sleep, too little effort, or too many exercise changes.

## Caveats

- Steele 2023 used one program (single sets, once a week); the plateau may come later with more volume, and the authors note this is unclear.
- Rates of progress differ a lot between individuals; no evidence-based "expected kg per month" exists, so the plugin should not promise one.
- Periodization trials are short and mostly measure 1RM; their relevance for general fitness goals is limited.
